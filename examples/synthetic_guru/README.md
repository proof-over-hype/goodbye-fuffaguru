# Example: a strategy that looks like a winner but does not beat chance

Everything here is **synthetic**: the market is a random walk generated by the code
(`fuffaguru.synthetic`), so no rule can have a real edge on it. No real trader, broker or market data.

```
python examples/synthetic_guru/run_example.py            # about 10-20 seconds
python examples/synthetic_guru/run_example.py --quick    # smaller, used by the tests
```

The story:

1. **The backtest.** A fictional guru tries 48 variants of "fade the first return to a swing level"
   (stop size, target, breakeven, trend filter) on about two months of 15-minute bars and keeps the best.
2. **The marketing.** He shows his 12 best trades plus one small loss, and quotes the share of days
   without a loss instead of the share of winning trades.
3. **The checks.** We write his rules down, freeze them (sha256 + closing rules), and run: random
   direction at the same instants, fake levels, the number of variants tried, a confirmation period
   three times longer that he never saw.

What you get (`sample_report.md` is the output of the full run):

- in sample: **214 trades, +0.22 R per trade after costs**; it even beats random direction (adjusted p = 0.024),
  because it was chosen on those very data;
- fake levels do almost as well (+0.16 R): the levels are not the reason;
- the result per trade falls from block to block;
- on new data: **about zero (-0.01 R)** on 664 trades;
- verdict: **NO EVIDENCE OF EDGE**.

That is the whole point of the method: an impressive backtest, a nice equity curve and a few chosen
examples are exactly what luck plus selection produce.
